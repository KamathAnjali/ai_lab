Increasing the amount of context can improve prediction because the model has more information about the words that came before the next word. A second-order model considers the previous two words, so it can distinguish between contexts that would look identical to a first-order model.

However, increasing the context also makes the conditional probability table much larger. A first-order model estimates P(next word | one previous word), while a second-order model estimates P(next word | two previous words). Therefore, there are many more possible contexts and many more probabilities to estimate.

With limited training data, many of these contexts may never appear in the training set. This results in zero-probability or poorly estimated probabilities.

Thus, more context can improve prediction when enough data is available, but it also increases the amount of data required to estimate the conditional probability table reliably.
