The conditional probability distribution P(next word | current word) is constructed by counting how often each word follows the current word and dividing by the total number of words that follow it.

the:
P(cat | the) = 3/6 = 0.5
P(dog | the) = 3/6 = 0.5

cat:
P(sat | cat) = 2/3 = 0.667
P(ran | cat) = 1/3 = 0.333

dog:
P(sat | dog) = 2/3 = 0.667
P(ran | dog) = 1/3 = 0.333

sat:
P(on | sat) = 4/4 = 1.0

ran:
P(to | ran) = 2/2 = 1.0

Zero-probability transitions are transitions that never occur in the training data. For example:

P(ran | the) = 0
P(on | cat) = 0
P(to | cat) = 0
P(sat | ran) = 0

In general, any word transition that does not appear in the training dataset has probability 0.
